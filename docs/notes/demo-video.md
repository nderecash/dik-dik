# Demo video: shot list and script

Draft for you to record. **I have not recorded anything.** This needs your voice and your
screen.

Target: **two minutes.** A PIRG reviewer will watch the first twenty seconds and decide
whether to watch the rest, so the twenty seconds have to carry the idea.

---

## The rule for this video

**Show the thing working before explaining what it is.**

The temptation with a project like this is to open with the argument, because the argument
is the good bit. Do not. Open with a person speaking to a machine and the machine doing it.
The argument lands much harder once someone has already seen it work.

## Shot list

| # | Time | What is on screen | What you say |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0:00–0:12 | Sector one. Rover still. You say "go left". Delay. It turns and drives. | Nothing. Let it play. Your voice is already in the shot. |
| 2 | 0:12–0:25 | Same shot continues. Comms panel visible: "I heard: go left", send bar filling, then "Salty: turning left". | "It waits until it is asked. Everything it hears, it shows you." |
| 3 | 0:25–0:45 | Arrive at a checkpoint. Rover stops itself, lamp goes orange, sweep runs, Control reports clean. | "The rover stops itself at each section. Landing a stop on a mark under a two and a half second delay is not a skill, it is a coin toss." |
| 4 | 0:45–1:00 | Cut to the same action on keyboard. Press W. Same delay. Same panel. | "Every command works on the keyboard, with the same delay on both, so neither is faster. That is not a courtesy mode." |
| 5 | 1:00–1:15 | Sector three, the night side. Cable's lit core the only readable thing. Toggle high contrast on and off. | "The cable is visible without turning on any accessibility setting. An object you need a setting to see would invert the whole argument." |
| 6 | 1:15–1:35 | The diagnostic blockage. Rover scans, Control offers cut, dissolve or push. You pick one. | "Once, the machine has an opinion and asks what you think. There are no wrong answers, and it says so." |
| 7 | 1:35–1:50 | Say "can you jump". Control replies that it does not jump, six wheels and no legs. | "It also answers things nobody asked it to." |
| 8 | 1:50–2:00 | Static on the comms panel, or a title card. | "Speech runs on your machine, offline. Nothing is sent anywhere and nothing is kept. I built a version that kept it, played it, and it felt like being watched." |

## Recording notes

**Capture at 1920x1080, 60fps** if OBS will give it to you. The follow camera pans a lot
and 30fps will smear.

**Record the game audio and your microphone on separate tracks.** You will want to balance
Control's voice against your own afterwards, and you cannot unmix them later. This is the
same mistake the in-game recording feature made and it is worth not repeating.

**Do the run several times and keep the one where you fluff a line.** A command that gets
misheard and then works on the second try is a better advert for this game than a clean
run, because the recovery is the design. If Whisper turns one of your "turn"s into "10" on
camera, keep it and say so.

**Do not narrate over your own commands.** Shot 1 needs silence around your voice so a
viewer can hear the game respond to it.

---

## The one thing to cut if you run long

Shot 7, the jump joke. It is the most fun and the least load-bearing. Everything else is
making an argument; that one is charm, and charm is what you cut first when a reviewer has
two minutes.
